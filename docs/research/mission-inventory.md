## 2. Detailed Mission Dossiers

### Mission 1: Astrobotic Peregrine Mission 1 (TO2-AB)
* **Contract / Task Order:** `TO2-AB` (awarded May 2019, contract value ~$79.5M, later adjusted to ~$108M).
* **Provider:** Astrobotic Technology (Pittsburgh, PA).
* **Lander:** Peregrine Lander (Class 1 small lander, ~90 kg payload capacity to surface).
* **Launch Date:** January 8, 2024 (02:18 EST) aboard ULA Vulcan Centaur (Cert-1 inaugural flight) from SLC-41, Cape Canaveral, FL.
* **Intended Landing Site:** Sinus Viscositatis (approx. 35.3° N, 40.9° W, near the Gruithuisen Domes).
* **Mission Status:** **Transit Failure / Controlled Atmospheric Re-entry**.
* **Manifested NASA Payloads (5):**
  1. `LRA` (Laser Retroreflector Array) - Passive laser ranging prism array (NASA GSFC).
  2. `NSS` (Neutron Spectrometer System) - Hydrogen and bulk regolith detector (NASA ARC).
  3. `LETS` (Linear Energy Transfer Spectrometer) - Radiation environment sensor (NASA JSC).
  4. `NIRVSS` (Near-Infrared Volatiles Spectrometer System) - Hydration & mineral composition (NASA ARC).
  5. `PITMS` (Peregrine Ion-Trap Mass Spectrometer) - Exosphere volatiles (NASA GSFC / ESA).
* **Flight Outcome & Investigation:**  
  Shortly after stage separation from Vulcan Centaur, Peregrine suffered a propulsion anomaly caused by a failure of a helium pressure control valve (PCV2) to reseat, overpressurizing a propellant tank and causing a structural rupture and persistent propellant leak. Astrobotic’s mission operations team stabilized the spacecraft using attitude control thrusters, oriented the solar arrays toward the Sun, and activated hosted payloads in translunar space.  
  *Scientific Return in Transit:* Both NASA's **LETS** and **NSS** instruments operated continuously for multiple days in cislunar space, returning valuable deep-space radiation and solar event data before Astrobotic commanded a safe, controlled re-entry into Earth's atmosphere over the South Pacific Ocean on January 18, 2024 (21:04 UTC).
* **Primary Source:** [NASA Science TO2-AB Overview](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/to2-astrobotic/) | [NASA Blog: Peregrine Mission Updates](https://blogs.nasa.gov/astrobotic-peregrine-mission-one/)

### Mission 2: Intuitive Machines IM-1 "Odysseus" (TO2-IM)
* **Contract / Task Order:** `TO2-IM` (awarded May 2019, contract value ~$77M, later adjusted to ~$118M).
* **Provider:** Intuitive Machines (Houston, TX).
* **Lander:** Nova-C class lander named *"Odysseus"* (Hexagonal cylinder with 6 landing legs, cryogenic liquid methane and liquid oxygen propulsion).
* **Launch Date:** February 15, 2024 (01:05 EST) on SpaceX Falcon 9 from LC-39A, Kennedy Space Center, FL.
* **Landing Date:** February 22, 2024 (18:23 EST / 23:23 UTC).
* **Target Landing Site:** Malapert A crater rim (approx. 80.13° S, 1.44° E), ~300 km from the lunar South Pole.
* **Actual Landing Site:** Confirmed at 80.1221° S, 1.4395° E by NASA Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera (LROC) at an elevation of -3,607 meters.
* **Mission Status:** **Landed / Historic Success (With Partial Lateral Tip)**. First American lunar landing since Apollo 17 (1972) and the **world's first successful commercial lunar landing**.
* **Manifested NASA Payloads (6):**
  1. `LN-1` (Lunar Node-1 Navigation Demonstrator) - Autonomous S-band positioning beacon (NASA MSFC).
  2. `SCALPSS` (Stereo Cameras for Lunar Plume-Surface Studies) - Descent plume photogrammetry (NASA LaRC).
  3. `ROLSES` (Radio-wave Observations at the Lunar Surface of the photo-Electron Sheath) - Low-frequency radio spectrometer (NASA GSFC).
  4. `NDL` (Navigation Doppler Lidar for Precise Velocity and Range Sensing) - Precision velocity and altimetry sensor (NASA LaRC).
  5. `RFMG` (Radio Frequency Mass Gauge) - Low-g cryogenic propellant gauging demo (NASA GRC).
  6. `LRA` (Laser Retroreflector Array) - 8 corner-cube prism permanent fiducial marker (NASA GSFC).
* **Flight Outcome & In-Flight Drama:**  
  Prior to descent orbit insertion, controllers discovered that the lander's primary commercial laser rangefinders could not fire because a physical safety disable pin had not been removed before launch. In an extraordinary real-time engineering response, NASA and Intuitive Machines engineers uploaded software patches to reroute altitude and velocity telemetry from NASA's experimental **NDL** payload directly into the flight guidance computer.  
  During the final moments of touchdown, Odysseus experienced excess lateral velocity, caught one of its landing pads on a lunar surface slope, fractured a landing strut, and came to rest tilted at approximately a 30-degree angle. Despite the tilt and compromised antenna alignment, all NASA active payloads established communication, operated on the surface, and downlinked scientific data before lunar nightfall on February 28, 2024.
* **Primary Source:** [NASA Science TO2-IM Overview](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/to2-im/) | [NASA IM-1 Mission Results Press Release](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-intuitive-machines-share-images-from-historic-moon-mission/)

### Mission 3: Firefly Aerospace Blue Ghost Mission 1 (TO19D)
* **Contract / Task Order:** `TO19D` (awarded February 2021, contract value ~$93.3M).
* **Provider:** Firefly Aerospace (Cedar Park, TX).
* **Lander:** Blue Ghost 1 (Monergol / bi-propellant lander, payload deck capacity ~150 kg).
* **Launch Vehicle:** SpaceX Falcon 9.
* **Target Landing Site:** Mare Crisium (approx. 18.5° N, 61.8° E), a dark, circular basaltic plain on the near side.
* **Mission Status:** **Delivered / Surface Operations**.
* **Manifested NASA Payloads (10 - Largest Single Scientific Manifest):**
  1. `NGLR` (Next Generation Lunar Retroreflector) - Deep lunar interior / cosmology ranging target (Univ. of Maryland / NASA GSFC).
  2. `RadPC` (Radiation Tolerant Computer System) - FPGA fault recovery processor with dosimeters (Montana State Univ.).
  3. `RAC` (Regolith Adherence Characterization) - Measures dust clinging to 30 material samples on rotating wheels (NASA Alpha / GRC).
  4. `LMS` (Lunar Magnetotelluric Sounder) - Electrometer & magnetometer measuring mantle electrical conductivity (Southwest Research Institute).
  5. `LEXI` (Lunar Environment Heliospheric X-ray Imager) - Soft X-ray imager observing Earth's magnetosphere (Boston Univ. / NASA GSFC).
  6. `LPV` (Lunar PlanetVac) - Pneumatic regolith sampling system using high-pressure gas jets (Honeybee Robotics).
  7. `LISTER` (Lunar Instrumentation for Subsurface Thermal Exploration with Rapidity) - 2-3 meter pneumatic heat flow probe (Texas Tech Univ.).
  8. `SCALPSS 1.1` (Stereo Cameras for Lunar Plume-Surface Studies) - Enhanced 6-camera stereo plume imaging suite (NASA LaRC).
  9. `EDS` (Electrodynamic Dust Shield) - Active electrostatic dust repeller on camera lenses and radiators (NASA KSC).
  10. `LuGRE` (Lunar GNSS Receiver Experiment) - GPS & Galileo signal reception at lunar distance (NASA GSFC / Italian Space Agency ASI).
* **Scientific Importance:**  
  Mare Crisium represents a major volcanic basaltic impact basin. Payloads investigate heat flow from the lunar mantle (`LISTER`), space weather interaction with Earth's magnetosphere (`LEXI`), and critical dust-repelling electrostatic technology (`EDS`).
* **Primary Source:** [NASA Science TO19D Overview](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/to19d-firefly/)

### Mission 4: Astrobotic Griffin Mission 1 (TO20A)
* **Contract / Task Order:** `TO20A` (awarded June 2020, contract value ~$199.5M, later expanded).
* **Provider:** Astrobotic Technology (Pittsburgh, PA).
* **Lander:** Griffin Lander (Medium-class lander, ~500 kg delivery capacity).
* **Launch Vehicle:** SpaceX Falcon Heavy from Kennedy Space Center, FL.
* **Target Destination:** Mons Mouton (approx. 84.9° S, 31.7° E, lunar South Pole region).
* **Mission Status:** **Planned Delivery Test & Lander Characterization**.
* **Programmatic Evolution & VIPER Cancellation:**  
  Originally awarded to deliver NASA’s flagship **VIPER** (Volatiles Investigating Polar Exploration Rover) rover to prospect for water ice. In July 2024, NASA announced the cancellation of the VIPER rover project due to cost growth and supply-chain schedule delays that threatened to disrupt the broader CLPS cadence. However, NASA elected to **continue the TO20A contract with Astrobotic as a Griffin flight demonstration**, utilizing a rover mass simulator and flight instrument test package to validate the heavy Griffin lander system and its propulsion without risking an uncrewed rover.
* **Primary Source:** [NASA Science TO20A Overview](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/to20a-astrobotic/) | [NASA VIPER Project Status Announcement](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/nasa-ends-viper-project-continues-moon-exploration/)

### Mission 5: Intuitive Machines IM-2 / PRIME-1
* **Contract / Task Order:** `PRIME-1` (Polar Resources Ice Mining Experiment-1, awarded October 2020, contract value ~$47M).
* **Provider:** Intuitive Machines (Houston, TX).
* **Lander:** Nova-C.
* **Launch Vehicle:** SpaceX Falcon 9 from LC-39A.
* **Target Destination:** Shackleton Connecting Ridge near the lunar South Pole (approx. 89.9° S).
* **Mission Status:** **Planned (Launch window 2025)**.
* **Manifested NASA Payloads (3 Core Scientific Instruments):**
  1. `TRIDENT` (The Regolith and Ice Drill for Exploring New Terrains) - 1-meter rotary percussive drill with internal temperature sensors (Honeybee Robotics).
  2. `MSolo` (Mass Spectrometer Observing Lunar Operations) - Modified quadrupole mass spectrometer analyzing volatile gases released from drill cuttings (NASA KSC).
  3. `LRA` (Laser Retroreflector Array) - Permanent laser ranging fiducial marker (NASA GSFC).
* **Key Demonstration:**  
  This mission represents NASA's **first direct attempt to drill and sample subsurface volatile ice on the Moon**. TRIDENT will incrementally drill down to 1 meter in 10-cm bites, depositing regolith on the surface where MSolo will sniff out water, carbon dioxide, ammonia, and methane before solar sublimation.
* **Primary Source:** [NASA Science PRIME-1 Overview](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/prime-1-im/)

### Mission 6: Intuitive Machines IM-3 (CP-11)
* **Contract / Task Order:** `CP-11` (PRISM-1A selection, awarded November 2021, contract value ~$77.5M).
* **Provider:** Intuitive Machines (Houston, TX).
* **Lander:** Nova-C.
* **Launch Vehicle:** SpaceX Falcon 9.
* **Target Destination:** Reiner Gamma (approx. 7.5° N, 59.0° W, Oceanus Procellarum).
* **Mission Status:** **Planned (Launch window 2026)**.
* **Manifested NASA Payloads (4 Suites / 8+ Discrete Instruments):**
  1. `Lunar Vertex PRISM-1 Suite` - Lander magnetometer, rover-mounted magnetometer, multispectral microscope, and plasma spectrometer (Johns Hopkins APL).
  2. `CADRE` (Cooperative Autonomous Distributed Robotic Exploration) - 3 shoe-box sized autonomous rovers demonstrating cooperative swarm exploration and 3D subsurface radar mapping (NASA JPL).
  3. `MoonRanger / Radiation Payload` - High-mobility micro-rover and KASI (Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute) space radiation dosimeter.
  4. `LRA` (Laser Retroreflector Array) - Passive laser ranging target (NASA GSFC).
* **Scientific Importance:**  
  Reiner Gamma is a prominent **lunar swirl**—a bright, sinuous surface marking coinciding with an anomalous localized crustal magnetic field ("mini-magnetosphere"). Lunar Vertex will measure whether the magnetic field shields the surface from solar wind ion weathering, testing the definitive hypothesis of lunar swirl genesis.
* **Primary Source:** [NASA Science CP-11 Overview](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/cp-11/)

### Mission 7: Team Draper APEX 1.0 (CP-12)
* **Contract / Task Order:** `CP-12` (PRISM-1B selection, awarded July 2022, contract value ~$73M).
* **Provider:** Team Draper (Cambridge, MA) as prime contractor, utilizing the **ispace-U.S. APEX 1.0 lander**.
* **Target Destination:** Schrödinger Basin on the lunar far side (approx. 75.0° S, 132.5° E).
* **Mission Status:** **Planned (Target 2026)**.
* **Manifested NASA Payloads (3 Suites):**
  1. `FSS` (FARSIDE Seismic Suite) - Two ultra-sensitive seismometers (VBB and SP) packaged inside a self-contained, vacuum-insulated thermal vault designed to operate across the freezing 14-day lunar night (NASA JPL / CNES).
  2. `LITMS` (Lunar Interior Temperature and Materials Suite) - Heat flow drill and magnetotelluric sounding probe (Southwest Research Institute).
  3. `LuSEE-Lite` (Lunar Surface Electromagnetics Experiment - Lite) - Radio and plasma wave monitor (UC Berkeley).
* **Scientific Importance:**  
  Schrödinger Basin is an enormous, pristine impact basin inside the South Pole-Aitken (SPA) Basin. FSS will record deep moonquakes on the far side for the first time in human history, shedding light on core-mantle boundary thickness and the disparity in crustal thickness between the near and far sides.
* **Primary Source:** [NASA Science CP-12 Overview](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/cp-12/)

### Mission 8: Firefly Blue Ghost Mission 2 (CS-3/4)
* **Contract / Task Order:** `CS-3/4` (awarded March 2023, contract value ~$112M).
* **Provider:** Firefly Aerospace (Cedar Park, TX).
* **Lander:** Blue Ghost 2 + Orbital Transfer Vehicle.
* **Target Destination:** Lunar Far Side surface & Lunar Orbit.
* **Mission Status:** **Planned (Target 2026)**.
* **Manifested NASA Payloads (3):**
  1. `LuSEE-Night` (Lunar Surface Electromagnetics Experiment - Night) - Low-frequency radio astronomy observatory developed jointly by NASA and the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE/Brookhaven/LBNL). Operates during lunar night on the far side—the most radio-quiet location in the inner solar system—to measure the 21-cm hydrogen cosmological signal from the cosmic "Dark Ages" prior to the ignition of the first stars.
  2. `Lunar Pathfinder` - Commercial lunar communications relay satellite developed by Surrey Satellite Technology Ltd (SSTL) with ESA, deployed into lunar orbit by Firefly to relay data from far-side and polar surface assets back to Earth.
  3. `User Terminal Payload` - Surface-to-orbit S-band communication transceiver.
* **Primary Source:** [NASA Science CS-3 Overview](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/cs-3/)
