## 2. Geological Deep-Dives by Site

### 1. Malapert A (Target of IM-1 / Odysseus)
* **Selenographic Setting:** A satellite crater adjacent to Malapert Mountain (Mons Malapert), located roughly 300 km from the lunar South Pole in the southern highlands.
* **Target Coordinates:** 80.13° S, 1.44° E.
* **Actual Confirmed Landing Coordinates:** **80.1221° S, 1.4395° E** (within 1.5 km of target, confirmed by NASA’s Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter Camera - LROC at an elevation of -3,607 meters).
* **Scientific Importance:**  
  The terrain consists of heavily cratered, ancient anorthositic crust dating back 4.4 billion years. High elevation points on the rim receive extended periods of sunlight, while nearby crater interiors lie in permanent shadow. It served as a critical operational proving ground for terminal descent navigation sensors (`NDL`) and plume-regolith dynamics (`SCALPSS`).
* **Source:** [NASA LROC Odysseus Landing Site Imagery](https://www.lroc.asu.edu/posts/1359)

### 2. Mare Crisium (Target of Blue Ghost 1)
* **Selenographic Setting:** An isolated, 555-kilometer-diameter circular impact basin on the northern near side, filled with multiple layers of volcanic flood basalts.
* **Coordinates:** 18.5° N, 61.8° E.
* **Scientific Importance:**  
  Because Crisium is a distinct mascon (mass concentration) basin separated from the Oceanus Procellarum KREEP terrane, measuring its subsurface heat flow with `LISTER` provides a clean baseline of the Moon's primordial thermal output without interference from radioactive thorium/uranium anomalies. Furthermore, Mare Crisium’s position on the eastern limb provides an unobstructed, high-angle view back toward Earth, enabling `LEXI` to take soft X-ray images of Earth’s dayside magnetopause.
* **Source:** [NASA Science Mare Crisium Delivery](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/to19d-firefly/)

### 3. Reiner Gamma (Target of IM-3 / CP-11)
* **Selenographic Setting:** Located on the smooth dark plains of Oceanus Procellarum. It is the most famous example of a **lunar swirl**—a bright, sinuous curvilinear feature that has no topographic elevation or depression.
* **Coordinates:** 7.5° N, 59.0° W.
* **The Mystery:** Swirls are not mountains or valleys; they are photometric markings directly correlated with strong localized crustal magnetic anomalies.
* **The Scientific Question:** Does the magnetic field create a "mini-magnetosphere" that deflects the positively charged solar wind (protons), preventing the space weathering that normally darkens exposed lunar soil? `Lunar Vertex` will place magnetometers and spectrometers on both the lander and a rover to navigate across the swirl boundary and measure the solar wind standoff in situ.
* **Source:** [NASA Science CP-11 Reiner Gamma](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/cp-11/)

### 4. Schrödinger Basin (Target of Draper APEX 1.0 / CP-12)
* **Selenographic Setting:** A pristine, 312-kilometer-wide impact basin within the vast South Pole-Aitken (SPA) Basin on the lunar far side.
* **Coordinates:** 75.0° S, 132.5° E.
* **Scientific Importance:**  
  1. **Seismic Far-Side Window:** All previous Apollo seismic stations were clustered on the near side. Deploying the `FARSIDE Seismic Suite (FSS)` inside Schrödinger allows scientists to record far-side seismic wave propagation, mapping the core-mantle boundary and resolving the Moon’s deep interior structure.
  2. **Mantle Exposure:** The giant impact penetrated through the thin far-side crust, excavating and exposing lower crust and upper mantle lithologies that can be investigated without deep core drilling.
* **Source:** [NASA Science CP-12 Schrödinger Basin](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/cp-12/)

### 5. Gruithuisen Domes (Target of Blue Ghost 3 / CP-21)
* **Selenographic Setting:** Two steep-sided volcanic mounds (Mons Gruithuisen Gamma and Delta) rising over 1,000 meters above the western Oceanus Procellarum plains.
* **Coordinates:** 36.0° N, 40.0° W.
* **The Geological Paradox:** On Earth, silicic (granitic/rhyolitic) volcanism requires two ingredients: plate tectonics and abundant water. The Moon has neither. Yet spectral data confirms that the Gruithuisen Domes are composed of silica-rich, viscous volcanic lava rather than typical iron-rich basalt. `Lunar-VISE` will climb the domes to analyze their geochemistry and unravel this fundamental planetary enigma.
* **Source:** [NASA Science CP-21 Gruithuisen Domes](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/cp-21-science/)

### 6. Mons Mouton & Shackleton Connecting Ridge (Lunar South Pole)
* **Mons Mouton:** Located at 84.9° S, 31.7° E. A broad, flat-topped lunar mountain named after Melba Roy Mouton, a pioneering African American mathematician and computer programmer at NASA. It features wide sunlit plateaus bordering deep craters that host permanent shadow, creating ideal thermal conditions for commercial landers and rover exploration.
* **Shackleton Connecting Ridge:** Located at ~89.9° S. A high-altitude ridge linking Shackleton crater with de Gerlache crater. It receives sunlight for over 85–90% of the lunar year, while its sheer drop-offs plunge into sub-40 Kelvin cold traps where water ice has remained frozen for billions of years.
* **Source:** [NASA CLPS Landing Sites Graphic (JSC-NP-2025-03-006)](https://www.nasa.gov/commercial-lunar-payload-services/landing-sites/)

## 3. Cartographic Precision, Datums & Uncertainties

* **Lunar Reference Coordinate System:**  
  All official coordinates are referenced to the **Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter (LRO) Lunar Orbiter Laser Altimeter (LOLA)** geodetic grid (Mean Earth/Polar Axis system, ULCN 2005 datum).
* **Targeting Dispersion Ellipses:**  
  Commercial landers typically target landing error ellipses ranging from $100\text{ m} \times 100\text{ m}$ (with active terrain relative navigation) to $1\text{ km} \times 1\text{ km}$ for earlier flights. The Odysseus landing at Malapert A demonstrated a touchdown accuracy within ~1.5 km of its intended center point.
* **Precision Uncertainties:**  
  For future missions, coordinates published in NASA task order solicitations represent **target landing zones** rather than pinpoint touchdown locations, which are finalized closer to flight after detailed high-resolution stereophotogrammetric hazard mapping by the provider.
