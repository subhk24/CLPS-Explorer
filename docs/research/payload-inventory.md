## 1. Payload Taxonomy & Science Classification

Every NASA payload delivered under CLPS addresses one or more foundational scientific questions defined by the National Academies Planetary Science Decadal Survey and the Artemis Science Plan. The instruments in this inventory are categorized into 8 core capability domains:

1. **Volatiles & Geochemistry (VG):** Detecting subsurface water ice, hydrated minerals, hydroxyl ($OH$), noble gases, and surface chemical composition.
2. **Geophysics & Interior Structure (GP):** Measuring heat flow from the lunar core, recording deep moonquakes, and quantifying magnetic and gravitational fields.
3. **Plume-Surface Interactions & Dust (PSI):** Imaging high-velocity engine exhaust blasting regolith, measuring dust levitation, and demonstrating active dust removal.
4. **Space Weather & Plasma Environment (SWP):** Mapping the photo-electron sheath, solar wind interactions, and cosmic ray radiation doses.
5. **Astrophysics & Cosmology (AC):** Probing the cosmological Dark Ages using low-frequency radio astronomy from the radio-quiet lunar far side.
6. **Precision Navigation, Timing & Geodesy (PNT):** Creating a permanent surface-based optical and radio beacon network for autonomous landing and orbital positioning.
7. **In-Situ Resource Utilization & Drilling (ISRU):** Autonomous mechanical and pneumatic extraction of regolith and ice samples.
8. **Extreme Environment Technology & Computing (TECH):** Testing radiation-hardened FPGA architectures, cryogenic fluid management, and autonomous multi-agent swarm robotics.

## 3. Deep-Dive Payload Profiles

### Profile 1: Navigation Doppler Lidar (NDL)
* **Lead Organization:** NASA Langley Research Center (Hampton, VA).
* **Principal Investigator:** Farzin Amzajerdian.
* **Instrument Architecture:** Fiber-optic coherent laser radar (LIDAR) deploying three narrow laser beams from an optical head connected via fiber cables to an electronics chassis. Operates at 1.55-micron eye-safe infrared wavelength.
* **Operating Principle:** Measures the line-of-sight Doppler frequency shift and laser round-trip time-of-flight to determine the lander's 3-axis velocity vectors (relative speed) and true altitude with sub-meter accuracy from 5 km above the surface to touchdown.
* **Historical Significance on IM-1:**  
  When Odysseus's onboard primary laser altimeters failed prior to lunar landing, flight controllers quickly uploaded an emergency software patch that fed NDL’s real-time velocity and altitude data directly into the lander’s closed-loop guidance system, preventing what would have been an unguided high-velocity crash landing.
* **Citations:** [NASA Langley NDL Technology Fact Sheet](https://www.nasa.gov/reference/navigation-doppler-lidar-ndl/) | [NASA Science TO2-IM NDL Profile](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/to2-im/)

### Profile 2: Polar Resources Ice Mining Experiment-1 (PRIME-1: TRIDENT + MSolo)
* **Lead Organizations:** Honeybee Robotics (TRIDENT drill) & NASA Kennedy Space Center (MSolo mass spectrometer).
* **Principal Investigators:** Kris Zacny (Honeybee) & Janine Captain (NASA KSC).
* **Instrument Architecture:** 
  * `TRIDENT` is a 1-meter rotary percussive drill with carbide cutting bits, a flute brush mechanism, and down-hole thermal sensors.
  * `MSolo` is a modified Commercial-Off-The-Shelf (COTS) quadrupole mass spectrometer enclosed in a vacuum-tight housing with a heated inlet line.
* **Operating Principle:** TRIDENT drills in 10-centimeter increments ("bites") to avoid frictional heating that could prematurely vaporize ice. Cuttings are brought to the surface and brushed onto a deposit pile. MSolo immediately samples evaporating molecules to detect and quantify water ($H_2O$), carbon monoxide ($CO$), carbon dioxide ($CO_2$), methane ($CH_4$), ammonia ($NH_3$), and hydrogen sulfide ($H_2S$).
* **Significance for Artemis:** This is the benchmark precursor ISRU mission directly informing human base camp placement and life-support resource estimates at the lunar South Pole.
* **Citations:** [NASA Science PRIME-1 Mission Profile](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/prime-1-im/)

### Profile 3: FARSIDE Seismic Suite (FSS)
* **Lead Organization:** NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (Pasadena, CA) in collaboration with CNES (France) and Imperial College London.
* **Principal Investigator:** Mark Panning (NASA JPL).
* **Instrument Architecture:** Packages two flight-proven seismometers—a Very Broadband (VBB) three-axis sensor (derived from the Mars InSight seismometer) and a Short-Period (SP) high-frequency sensor—inside an insulated spherical vacuum chamber with dedicated primary batteries and radiative cooling.
* **Survival Breakthrough:** The Moon experiences 14 continuous Earth-days of night with temperatures plunging below $-180^\circ\text{C}$ (93 K). The commercial lander will power down and die during the first night, but FSS is designed as an autonomous station with enough battery reserve to **survive and operate continuously for months across lunar nights**.
* **Scientific Question:** All Apollo seismometers were deployed on the near side. FSS will record the first-ever seismic data from the lunar far side inside Schrödinger Basin, detecting deep moonquakes generated by Earth-Moon tidal forces, recording meteorite impact rates, and determining if the Moon's far-side mantle is geophysically asymmetric.
* **Citations:** [NASA Science CP-12 FSS Profile](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/cp-12/) | [JPL FARSIDE Seismic Suite Mission Page](https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/missions/farside-seismic-suite-fss)

### Profile 4: LuSEE-Night (Lunar Surface Electromagnetics Experiment - Night)
* **Lead Organization:** NASA Science Mission Directorate & U.S. Department of Energy (DOE - Brookhaven National Laboratory / Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory).
* **Principal Investigator:** Stuart D. Bale (UC Berkeley).
* **Instrument Architecture:** Four 3-meter storable tubular extendable member (STEM) monopole antennas deploying in an orthogonal cross configuration, connected to ultra-low-noise preamplifiers and digital spectrometers.
* **Scientific Question:** The early universe between 380,000 years and 400 million years after the Big Bang (before the first stars formed) is known as the **Cosmological Dark Ages**. The only direct probe of this era is the highly redshifted 21-centimeter neutral hydrogen line, now resting in the low-frequency radio spectrum (0.5 to 50 MHz).
* **Why the Lunar Far Side is Required:** Terrestrial radio broadcasts, cell towers, and ionospheric plasma bounce make this measurement completely impossible from Earth or Earth orbit. The Moon’s far side is the only radio-quiet shield in the inner solar system. By operating during the lunar night, LuSEE-Night eliminates solar radio noise, unlocking the first cosmological window into the infant universe.
* **Citations:** [NASA Science CS-3 Overview](https://science.nasa.gov/lunar-science/clps-deliveries/cs-3/) | [Brookhaven National Lab LuSEE-Night Project](https://www.bnl.gov/lusee-night/)
