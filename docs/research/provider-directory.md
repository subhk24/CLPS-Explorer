## 1. The CLPS Commercial Delivery Architecture

Under traditional government aerospace contracting (e.g., Federal Acquisition Regulation Part 15 Cost-Plus contracts), NASA designed the spacecraft architecture, directed engineering subcontractors, owned the flight hardware, assumed full operational risk, and paid for all budget overruns.

Under the **Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS)** model:
* **Contract Vehicle:** Multiple-award Indefinite-Delivery/Indefinite-Quantity (IDIQ) contracts.
* **Pricing Model:** Firm-Fixed-Price (FFP) Task Orders.
* **Combined Program Ceiling:** $2.6 billion through November 2028.
* **Scope of Service:** End-to-end commercial lunar delivery services. The contractor is responsible for:
  1. Lunar lander design, qualification, and manufacturing.
  2. NASA payload integration, safety reviews, and environmental testing.
  3. Securing commercial launch services (e.g., SpaceX Falcon 9 / Falcon Heavy, ULA Vulcan Centaur).
  4. Cislunar transit trajectory, deep space communications, and orbital insertion.
  5. Autonomous hazard-detection descent and lunar soft touchdown.
  6. Surface power and communications relay for the required mission duration (typically one lunar day / ~14 Earth days).
* **Commercial Freedom & Co-Manifesting:** Contractors are encouraged to sell unused mass and volume on their landers to international space agencies (e.g., ESA, ASI, KASI), academic institutions, and commercial customers. This fosters a self-sustaining cislunar marketplace.

## 2. The CLPS Contractor Pool: 14 Selected Vendors

NASA built the vendor pool through two competitive solicitations:
* **November 29, 2018 (Original Pool of 9):** Astrobotic Technology, Deep Space Systems, Draper, Firefly Aerospace, Intuitive Machines, Lockheed Martin Space, Masten Space Systems, Moon Express, Orbit Beyond.
* **November 18, 2019 (Pool Expansion of 5):** Blue Origin, Ceres Robotics, Sierra Nevada Corporation (now Sierra Space), SpaceX, Tyvak Nano-Satellite Systems.

### Active Pool Status (13 Active, 1 Defunct):
* 13 companies remain eligible to bid on future task orders.
* **Masten Space Systems** declared Chapter 11 bankruptcy in July 2022; its assets and intellectual property (including the XL-1 lander design) were acquired by Astrobotic Technology.
* **Orbit Beyond** was awarded an initial delivery in May 2019 but voluntarily terminated the contract in July 2019 citing internal corporate reorganization, returning to the eligible pool for future bids.

## 3. Profiles of the Five Awarded Contractors

### 1. Intuitive Machines (Houston, TX)
* **Company Profile:** Founded in 2013 by Stephen Altemus, Kam Ghaffarian, and Tim Crain. Publicly traded on NASDAQ (`LUNR`).
* **Lander Series:**
  * **Nova-C:** Class 1 lander (~100–130 kg payload capacity to surface). Utilizes a proprietary 3D-printed VR900 engine burning cryogenic liquid oxygen ($LOX$) and liquid methane ($LCH_4$)—the first spacecraft to employ methalox propulsion on a lunar trajectory.
  * **Nova-D:** Medium/heavy cargo lander under development (~500 kg to 1,000 kg capacity) utilizing multiple VR900 engines.
* **Awarded Task Orders (5):**
  1. `TO2-IM` (IM-1 / *Odysseus*): Landed at Malapert A on Feb 22, 2024. Delivered 6 NASA payloads. First commercial Moon landing in history.
  2. `PRIME-1` (IM-2): Polar Resources Ice Mining Experiment at Shackleton Connecting Ridge.
  3. `CP-11` (IM-3): Delivery of Lunar Vertex and CADRE swarm rovers to Reiner Gamma swirl.
  4. `CP-22` (IM-4): Delivery of 6 PRISM-2 science payloads to Mons Mouton.
  5. `CT-4`: Nova-D heavy cargo delivery to lunar South Pole.
* **Key Competitive Strengths:** Flight-proven cryogenic propulsion, rapid launch cadence, operational flight experience, direct ground station network (Nova Control).

### 2. Firefly Aerospace (Cedar Park, TX)
* **Company Profile:** Private aerospace company specializing in launch vehicles (Alpha rocket) and lunar landers.
* **Lander Series:**
  * **Blue Ghost 1:** Highly compact, efficient bi-propellant lander (~155 kg payload capacity to the lunar surface).
  * **Blue Ghost 2 & Orbital Transfer Stage:** Enhanced platform with an upper-stage transfer vehicle capable of orbital satellite deployment before descending to the surface.
* **Awarded Task Orders (4):**
  1. `TO19D` (Blue Ghost 1): Delivered 10 NASA payloads to Mare Crisium (largest single science payload manifest in CLPS history).
  2. `CS-3/4` (Blue Ghost 2): Far-side delivery carrying LuSEE-Night radio telescope and deploying ESA’s Lunar Pathfinder relay satellite into lunar orbit.
  3. `CP-21` (Blue Ghost 3): Targeted delivery of the Lunar-VISE suite to the volcanic Gruithuisen Domes.
  4. `CS-6` (Blue Ghost 4): Dedicated polar science delivery.
* **Key Competitive Strengths:** Heavy payload margin, high structural stability, integrated orbital transfer capability.

### 3. Astrobotic Technology (Pittsburgh, PA)
* **Company Profile:** Founded in 2007 by Carnegie Mellon roboticist Red Whittaker to compete for the Google Lunar XPRIZE.
* **Lander Series:**
  * **Peregrine:** Small lander (~90 kg payload capacity) powered by hypergolic bi-propellant engines.
  * **Griffin:** Medium-heavy lander (~500 kg surface payload capacity) designed to carry heavy rovers and infrastructure.
* **Awarded Task Orders (2):**
  1. `TO2-AB` (Peregrine Mission 1): Launched Jan 8, 2024. Propellant valve rupture prevented lunar landing. Spacecraft returned deep-space science data before controlled re-entry.
  2. `TO20A` (Griffin Mission 1): Originally manifested for the VIPER rover. Following VIPER’s cancellation, maintained by NASA as a critical heavy-lander flight qualification test to Mons Mouton.
* **Key Competitive Strengths:** Pioneer in commercial lunar payload integration, deep robotics heritage from CMU, advanced terrestrial test facilities.

### 4. Team Draper (Cambridge, MA)
* **Company Profile:** Charles Stark Draper Laboratory (famed for Apollo guidance, navigation, and control systems) serving as prime contractor in a commercial consortium with **ispace-U.S.**, General Atomics, and Systima.
* **Lander Series:**
  * **APEX 1.0:** Designed and manufactured by ispace-U.S. (Denver, CO), capable of carrying ~300–500 kg to the lunar surface.
* **Awarded Task Orders (1):**
  1. `CP-12`: High-priority delivery to Schrödinger Basin on the lunar far side, carrying the FARSIDE Seismic Suite (FSS) and LITMS heat flow probe.
* **Key Competitive Strengths:** Decades of mission-critical GN&C (Guidance, Navigation, and Control) engineering combined with ispace's dedicated lander manufacturing infrastructure.

### 5. Blue Origin (Kent, WA)
* **Company Profile:** Aerospace company founded by Jeff Bezos. Prime contractor for the Artemis Human Landing System (HLS) Sustaining Lander (Blue Moon MK2).
* **Lander Series:**
  * **Blue Moon MK1:** Single-launch, uncrewed commercial cargo lander capable of delivering up to 3,000 kg (3 metric tons) directly to the lunar surface using liquid hydrogen and liquid oxygen ($LH_2/LOX$) BE-7 cryogenic engines.
* **Awarded Task Orders (1):**
  1. `CT-3`: Space Technology Mission Directorate delivery carrying cryogenic fluid management and precision landing demonstrations to the South Pole.
* **Key Competitive Strengths:** Unrivaled heavy payload capacity (3 metric tons), long-duration cryogenic BE-7 engine technology, integration with New Glenn launch vehicle.
