## 1. Executive Summary: What is CLPS?

**Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS)** is an initiative established by NASA's Science Mission Directorate (SMD) in collaboration with the Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) and the Exploration Systems Development Mission Directorate (ESDMD). 

Introduced in 2018 as a core capability supporting the **Artemis campaign**, CLPS completely inverts NASA’s historical paradigm for robotic planetary exploration:

* **Traditional Model (Cost-Plus / NASA-Owned):** Historically (e.g., Surveyor, Apollo, Curiosity, Perseverance), NASA specified every engineering requirement, owned the spacecraft design, oversaw fabrication, procured the launch vehicle, and operated the spacecraft throughout its mission lifecycle.
* **The CLPS Commercial Model (Firm-Fixed-Price / Vendor-Provided Service):** Under CLPS, NASA acts as **one of many commercial customers purchasing a delivery service**. Commercial vendors are responsible for end-to-end mission delivery: designing and manufacturing the lunar lander, integrating NASA and non-NASA payloads, securing the commercial launch vehicle, conducting transit navigation and communications, executing the lunar landing, and supporting surface operations.

> **NASA Official Stance on Risk Posture:**  
> *"Landing on the Moon is hard. With any new endeavor there is a new level of risk. With CLPS, NASA has accepted the risk that going through this innovative approach with commercial companies, that there could be some failures. Even with a few risks, CLPS remains an affordable model to conduct frequent lunar surface exploration."*  
> — Source: [NASA Commercial Lunar Payload Services Overview](https://www.nasa.gov/commercial-lunar-payload-services/)

## 2. Verification of the Starting Research Lead: "60+ Payloads / Instruments"

### The Exact Official Statement
NASA's official program metric page and primary CLPS documentation state verbatim:

> **"To date, NASA has awarded 17 lunar deliveries to five CLPS vendors to carry more than 60 payloads to the Moon. CLPS contracts are indefinite delivery, indefinite quantity contracts with a combined maximum contract value of $2.6 billion through November 2028."**  
> — Source: [NASA Commercial Lunar Payload Services](https://www.nasa.gov/commercial-lunar-payload-services/), modified July 2026 / reviewed October 2026.

### What the Official Data Actually Counts (Methodology Breakdown)

To maintain absolute scientific and statistical rigor, CLPS Explorer establishes clear boundaries for this metric:

1. **"60+ Payloads" represents NASA-funded Science and Technology Payloads:**  
   It counts individual instruments, technology demonstrators, and sensor suites funded through NASA solicitations—predominantly **NPLSI** (NASA-Provided Lunar Science Payloads), **LSITP** (Lunar Surface Instrument and Technology Programs), and the **PRISM** (Payloads and Research Investigations on the Surface of the Moon) calls (PRISM-1A, PRISM-1B, PRISM-2).
2. **It does NOT count commercial or non-NASA payloads:**  
   Commercial landers carry dozens of commercial payloads (e.g., Celestis memorial capsules, artworks, university cubesats, commercial rovers like Lunar Outpost MAPP, and private communication nodes like Nokia’s Bell Labs 4G/LTE demo). These are not included in the "60+ NASA payloads" count.
3. **It reflects Manifested and Awarded Task Orders:**  
   The "60+" figure is a **cumulative programmatic total** of instruments awarded across the 17 commercial task orders. It spans completed missions (e.g., 5 on Peregrine TO2-AB, 6 on Odysseus TO2-IM), missions currently launched or delivered (e.g., 10 on Blue Ghost 1 TO19D), and planned missions manifested through 2028 (e.g., PRIME-1, CP-11, CP-12, CS-3/4, CP-21, CP-22, CT-3, CS-6, CT-4).
4. **Instrument Suite Packaging vs. Discrete Sensors:**  
   Certain NASA payloads are complex multi-sensor suites that NASA counts as a single manifested payload delivery. For example:
   * **Lunar Vertex** (manifested on CP-11 to Reiner Gamma) includes a lander magnetometer, a rover magnetometer, a multispectral microscope, and a plasma spectrometer. In high-level summaries, NASA refers to it as one PRISM payload suite, but in detailed instrument catalogs, it represents 4 distinct scientific instruments.
   * **FARSIDE Seismic Suite (FSS)** (manifested on CP-12 to Schrödinger Basin) contains two distinct sensors: a Very Broadband (VBB) seismometer and a Short Period (SP) seismometer within an autonomous survival container.
5. **Reallocations Across Missions:**  
   Instruments are occasionally reallocated due to lander mass margins or mission adjustments. For example, the **MSolo** (Mass Spectrometer Observing Lunar Operations) was originally slated for Astrobotic's Peregrine (TO2-AB), reallocated to preserve descent margins, and manifested on PRIME-1 (IM-2) and the VIPER instrument suite. Our dataset tracks hardware heritage and flight manifestations separately to avoid double-counting.

## 4. Why CLPS is Essential to the Artemis Program

CLPS is not an isolated science program; it is the **robotic scouting and technological vanguard of the Artemis campaign**:

1. **Strategic Prospecting of Lunar Volatiles (Water Ice):**  
   Human presence at the lunar South Pole depends upon In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU)—mining water ice trapped in Permanently Shadowed Regions (PSRs) for life support and propellant (liquid hydrogen/oxygen). Missions like **PRIME-1** (TRIDENT drill + MSolo mass spectrometer) verify the physical state, depth, and concentration of subsurface volatiles before crews arrive.
2. **Plume-Surface Interaction (PSI) Characterization:**  
   Large human landing systems (such as Starship HLS and Blue Origin Blue Moon) will blast tons of high-velocity rocket exhaust at the lunar surface during landing. Instruments like **SCALPSS** (Stereo Cameras for Lunar Plume-Surface Studies) measure high-speed regolith erosion and cratering to protect future landing sites and base infrastructure.
3. **Planetary Protection & Regolith Mitigation:**  
   Lunar dust is abrasive, electrostatic, and chemically reactive. Payloads like **RAC** (Regolith Adherence Characterization) and **EDS** (Electrodynamic Dust Shield) quantify dust adhesion on spacesuits, solar panels, and optical lenses, validating active electrostatic removal technologies.
4. **Surface Geodesy & Autonomous Navigation Infrastructure:**  
   Payloads like **LRA** (Laser Retroreflector Array), **NDL** (Navigation Doppler Lidar), and **LN-1** (Lunar Node-1 navigation beacon) establish an autonomous surface-based positioning, navigation, and timing (PNT) network—the foundation of a lunar GPS.
5. **Decadal Survey High-Priority Science:**  
   CLPS addresses long-standing planetary science goals identified in the National Academies Planetary Science Decadal Survey, including far-side low-frequency radio astronomy (LuSEE-Night), heat flow probing of the deep lunar mantle (LISTER, LITMS), and testing crustal magnetic anomalies (Lunar Vertex at Reiner Gamma).

## 5. Summary of Task Order Naming Taxonomy

NASA uses a structured task order nomenclature that reflects the evolving maturity of the program:
* **Legacy Task Orders (2018–2020):** Named by contract sequence or fiscal year (e.g., `TO2-AB` = Task Order 2, Astrobotic; `TO2-IM` = Task Order 2, Intuitive Machines; `TO19D` = 2019 Task Order D; `TO20A` = 2020 Task Order A).
* **PRISM Science Deliveries (`CP-#`):** Payloads and Research Investigations on the Surface of the Moon. For example, `CP-11` designates the 1st selection from the PRISM-1 solicitation; `CP-12` designates the 2nd selection; `CP-21` designates the 1st selection from PRISM-2.
* **Science Mission Directorate Deliveries (`CS-#`):** Direct SMD-managed science missions (e.g., `CS-3/4` for Firefly lunar far-side delivery; `CS-6` for polar delivery; `CS-8` for Moon Base science).
* **Space Technology Mission Directorate Deliveries (`CT-#`):** Technology demonstration missions focusing on autonomy, cryogenic handling, and landing systems (e.g., `CT-3` Blue Origin; `CT-4` Intuitive Machines Nova-D).
* **Exploration Systems Deliveries (`CX-#`):** Large cargo and surface infrastructure deliveries (e.g., `CX-2` delivering Lunar Terrain Vehicles).
